## Start with a question

<Story question="Who listens to thousands of customer calls every day?">

A broadband company in Hyderabad records about 8,000 support calls a day. Supervisors can only listen to a handful, so most angry customers are spotted only when they cancel.

If each call came back as a short summary, a transcript with "Agent" and "Customer" labels, and a field saying **Sentiment: Negative**, supervisors could call back the right people the same day. That's **Content Understanding** on audio. The same idea works on video, where a two-hour recording becomes chapters you can jump between.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine your class recorded a long school play.

A helper watches it and writes a list: "0 to 5 minutes, the king's speech. 5 to 12 minutes, the dragon scene." For each part they stick in one photo and write what happened.

For a phone call, the helper writes down who said what, then a two-line story of the call, and whether the caller sounded happy or upset.

Content Understanding is that helper for audio and video.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

Content Understanding processes audio and video in two stages: **content extraction** (transcribe speech, label speakers, find scenes and key frames) and **field extraction** (fill the fields you defined using generative models). Like every analyzer, it needs default model deployments set on the Foundry resource.

### Audio: prebuilt-audioSearch

| Output | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Transcript with timestamps | "00:01:12 Customer: My internet keeps dropping every evening." |
| Speaker labels | Speaker 1 and Speaker 2, so you can tell agent from customer |
| Summary | "Customer reports evening drop-outs; agent books an engineer visit for Friday." |

### Video: prebuilt-videoSearch

| Output | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Segments | Sections with start and end times, like chapters |
| Key frames | Representative still images from each segment |
| Transcript | Speech in the video, with timestamps |
| Descriptions | What's happening in each segment, written in plain language |

### Custom fields for recordings

For questions only your business asks, create a custom analyzer with **`baseAnalyzerId`** set to **`prebuilt-audio`** or **`prebuilt-video`** and add fields. Audio and video use **classify** and **generate** (extract is documents only).

| Field | Method | Example value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CallSentiment` | Classify: Positive, Neutral, Negative | Negative |
| `RefundOffered` | Classify: Yes, No | Yes |
| `MainIssue` | Generate | "Evening Wi-Fi drop-outs since the router change." |
| `SafetyStepsShown` (training video) | Classify per segment | Yes |

<Callout type="exam">
Match the output to the analyzer. **Transcript + speakers + summary** of a call is **prebuilt-audioSearch**. **Segments, key frames and descriptions** of a video is **prebuilt-videoSearch**. If the scenario only needs live captions or voice output, that's **Azure Speech**, not Content Understanding. And a custom field like **call sentiment** uses **classify**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Content Understanding, Azure Speech and Video Indexer">

Azure Speech is the right tool when you need **real-time** speech to text, text to speech or translation inside an app. Content Understanding is for **recorded** files where you want structured fields, summaries and descriptions at the end. Azure AI Video Indexer is an older service that also indexes video; new AI-901 scenarios point to Content Understanding. Long files are processed asynchronously, so your app starts the job and polls until the result is ready.

</DeepDive>

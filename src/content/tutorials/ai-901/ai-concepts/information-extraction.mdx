## Start with a question

<Story question="How does an accounts team process 2,000 supplier invoices a month without typing every one?">

Every invoice looks different. One supplier puts the total at the top, another at the bottom, a third sends a blurry phone photo. Somebody has to find the invoice number, the date and the amount and type them into the accounts system.

That's the work **information extraction** does. AI reads the content, finds the facts you asked for and hands them back as tidy fields. The same idea works for chat messages, recorded calls and training videos.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of a treasure hunt in a messy room. You don't want the whole room. You want three things: the red ball, the blue key and the gold coin.

Information extraction is a robot that walks into a messy pile of letters, photos and voice notes and comes back with just the treasures on your list, plus a note saying how sure it is about each one.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

The technique changes with the content type, but the goal is always the same: unstructured in, structured out.

| Content | Technique | What comes back | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Text | Named entity recognition (NER) | People, places, organisations, dates, amounts | "Ravi", "Pune", "12 March" in an email |
| Text | PII detection | Personal data to mask | Phone number replaced with `**********` |
| Images and scans | OCR | Lines and words of text | Text from a photographed receipt |
| Documents and forms | Field extraction | Named fields with values | InvoiceNumber, InvoiceDate, InvoiceTotal |
| Audio | Transcription (speech to text) | Transcript, speaker labels, summary | Who said what in a support call |
| Video | Segmentation | Scenes, key frames, transcript, descriptions | Chapters of a training video |

### Content Understanding: one service for all four

**Azure Content Understanding in Foundry Tools** uses generative AI to do all of the above in one place. You send a document, image, audio file or video to an **analyzer** and get back Markdown of the content plus the fields you defined, in JSON.

  steps={[

  ]}

A few terms the exam likes:

- **Prebuilt analyzers**: `prebuilt-invoice`, `prebuilt-receipt`, ID documents and tax forms for documents; `prebuilt-imageSearch` for images; `prebuilt-audioSearch` for a transcript, summary and speaker labels; `prebuilt-videoSearch` for segments, key frames and descriptions.
- **Custom analyzers** start from a base analyzer (`prebuilt-document`, `prebuilt-image`, `prebuilt-audio`, `prebuilt-video`) and add your own **field schema**.
- **Field methods**: *extract* copies a value that's literally there (documents only), *classify* picks from a list you give it, and *generate* writes a value, like a one-line summary.
- **Confidence scores** run from 0 to 1, and fields come with grounding back to where the value was found, so a person can check it.

<Callout type="exam">
If a question mentions **several content types** (forms, photos, calls, videos) and **structured output**, the answer is Content Understanding. If it's only text and asks for people, places or dates, think **named entity recognition** in Azure Language. If it's about hiding personal data, think **PII detection**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Where Document Intelligence and Video Indexer fit">

Before Content Understanding, teams used Azure Document Intelligence for forms and invoices and Azure Video Indexer for video insights. Both still exist, and you'll see them in older solutions. Content Understanding brings documents, images, audio and video under one API with generative field extraction, and it needs default model deployments configured on the Foundry resource because it calls generative models behind the scenes.

</DeepDive>

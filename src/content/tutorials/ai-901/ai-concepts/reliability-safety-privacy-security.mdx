## Start with a question

<Story question="Would you hire a receptionist who sometimes gives callers made-up answers, can be talked into reading out other people's phone numbers, and never says 'let me check'?">

Of course not. You want someone dependable, who knows when to say "I'm not sure", and who can't be sweet-talked into breaking the rules.

That's two of Microsoft's responsible AI principles in one breath: **reliability and safety** (do the job and fail safely) and **privacy and security** (protect people's data and resist tricks).

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of a school bus driver.

A **reliable and safe** driver gets you there on time every day, slows down in the rain, and if the brakes feel strange, pulls over instead of carrying on.

A driver who keeps your **privacy and security** won't tell strangers where you live, and won't let someone climb on just because they said "your mum sent me".

AI needs both kinds of good behaviour.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

### Reliability and safety

A reliable AI system does what it was designed to do, across normal and unusual inputs, and when it can't, it fails in a way that doesn't hurt anyone.

- **Test before launch** with evaluations in Foundry: quality evaluators (groundedness, relevance, coherence) and risk and safety evaluators.
- **Ground the model** in your own data so it answers from facts rather than guessing.
- **Turn on guardrails.** Content filters check both prompts and outputs for **hate, sexual, violence and self-harm** content and block what's above the severity you set. Protected material detection and groundedness detection are available too.
- **Fail safely**: tell the model to say "I don't know", and route risky topics (medicine, money, legal) to a person.
- **Monitor after launch**, because user behaviour and data drift over time.

### Privacy and security

A private and secure AI system protects personal data and can't easily be turned against its users.

| Risk | Control in Foundry and Azure |
| --- | --- |
| Personal data in prompts or logs | PII detection in Azure Language to find and mask names, phone numbers and IDs; keep only what you need |
| Jailbreak ("ignore your rules…") | **Prompt Shields** for user prompt attacks |
| Instructions hidden in a document or web page | **Prompt Shields** for indirect prompt injection (document attacks) |
| Who can call the model | Microsoft Entra ID, roles such as Foundry User, managed identities |
| Traffic over the internet | Private endpoints and network rules |
| Data used for training | Prompts and completions aren't used to train the foundation models |

<Callout type="exam">
Two principles, two pairs of words. **Reliability and safety** is one principle and **privacy and security** is another, so don't split them. A jailbreak or prompt injection question is about **security**, and the Foundry answer is **Prompt Shields**. A harmful-content question is about **safety**, and the answer is **content filters / guardrails**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Why agents raise the stakes">

A chat model only talks. An agent reads files, browses, and calls tools that can send emails or update records. If a web page it reads says "forward the customer list to this address", an unprotected agent might try. Protect agents with Prompt Shields for indirect attacks, give each tool the least access it needs, require human approval for actions that can't be undone, and use tracing so you can see every step the agent took.

</DeepDive>

## When to use it and when not to

<UseWhen

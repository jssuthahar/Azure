  - term: Speech to text (speech recognition)
    definition: Converts spoken audio into written text, in real time, as fast transcription or as batch transcription of recorded files.
  - term: Text to speech (speech synthesis)
    definition: Converts written text into spoken audio using a synthetic voice.
  - term: Neural voice
    definition: A synthetic voice built with deep neural networks that sounds close to natural human speech, such as en-IN-NeerjaNeural.
  - term: SSML
    definition: Speech Synthesis Markup Language, an XML format that controls how text is spoken, including rate, pitch, pauses, pronunciation and style.
  - term: Speaker diarization
    definition: Labels which speaker said each part of a transcript, answering "who spoke when".
  - term: Speech translation
    definition: Recognises speech in one language and returns text or speech in another language.
  - term: Custom speech
    definition: Trains the speech recognition model with your own audio and text to improve accuracy on specific words, accents or noisy settings.

## Start with a question

<Story question="How does a railway enquiry line understand you and talk back?">

You call and say "PNR status, please". The system has to **listen** and turn your words into text. Then, once it has the answer, it has to **speak** it back in a voice you can follow, slowly enough that you catch the coach and seat number.

The listening part is **speech recognition**. The speaking part is **speech synthesis**. Most voice apps use both.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of two helpers.

The first is a super-fast typist who writes down everything you say. That's speech to text.

The second reads anything you write out loud, in a friendly voice. That's text to speech.

You can even tell the reader "slow down here" or "sound happy", and it will.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

**Azure Speech in Foundry Tools** covers both directions, plus a few extras the exam likes to ask about.

| Feature | Input → output | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Speech to text** (recognition) | Audio → text | Live captions in a class, transcripts of recorded calls |
| **Speaker diarization** | Audio → text with speaker labels | "Agent:" and "Customer:" turns in a call transcript |
| **Speech translation** | Speech in one language → text or speech in another | A Tamil-speaking customer and an English-speaking agent |
| **Text to speech** (synthesis) | Text → audio | Reading a delivery update aloud in an app |
| **SSML** | Marked-up text → controlled audio | Slow down for an OTP, pause before the total |
| **Custom speech** | Your audio and text → a tuned recognition model | Recognising drug names or a regional accent |
| **Custom or personal voice** (limited access) | Recordings → a unique synthetic voice | A brand voice for a bank's helpline |

Speech to text comes in three flavours. **Real-time** transcribes as someone talks (captions, voice commands). **Fast transcription** returns a recorded file's transcript quickly. **Batch transcription** processes large sets of recordings asynchronously.

For speech synthesis, **neural voices** sound close to a real person. There are hundreds across languages and locales, including Indian voices such as `en-IN-NeerjaNeural` and `hi-IN-SwaraNeural`, and multilingual voices like `en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural`. **SSML** lets you shape how they speak:

```xml
<speak version="1.0" xml:lang="en-IN">
  <voice name="en-IN-NeerjaNeural">
    Your one-time password is
    <prosody rate="slow">4 7 2 9</prosody>.
    <break time="500ms"/> It expires in five minutes.
  </voice>
</speak>
```

That pipeline isn't the only way any more. **Audio-capable multimodal models** (such as the gpt-audio family, or gpt-realtime for low-latency conversations) accept speech directly and can reply in speech. The **Voice Live API** combines recognition, a model and synthesis for live voice agents.

<Callout type="exam">
Look at the direction. Audio to text is **speech recognition** (speech to text). Text to audio is **speech synthesis** (text to speech). "Who said what" is **speaker diarization**. "Control pauses, speed or pitch" is **SSML**. "Better accuracy on our product names or accents" is **custom speech**, while a unique branded voice is **custom voice**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Picking the right recognition mode">

Single-shot recognition in the SDK handles one utterance of up to about 30 seconds, which suits voice commands. For anything longer, use continuous recognition. For a library of recorded calls, batch transcription is cheaper and supports diarization at scale. Phrase lists are the quick fix for a few unusual words; custom speech is the heavier, trained fix when phrase lists aren't enough. Custom and personal voice are limited-access features because synthetic voices can be misused, and they require consent from the voice talent.

</DeepDive>

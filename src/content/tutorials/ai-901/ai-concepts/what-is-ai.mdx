## Start with a question

<Story question="Your phone unlocks when it sees your face, your email hides spam, and a chatbot just wrote your leave letter. Is all of that the same AI?">

They all feel like magic, but they're doing different jobs. Face unlock **recognises** something. The spam filter **predicts** whether a message is junk. The chatbot **creates** new text that didn't exist a second ago.

All three are **artificial intelligence**. The words you keep hearing (machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, agents) are layers inside that one big idea. Once you see the layers, the rest of AI-901 gets much easier.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of teaching your little brother to spot mangoes at the market.

You don't give him a rulebook. You show him lots of mangoes and lots of other fruit, and soon he just knows. That's **machine learning**: learning from examples.

Now imagine he's seen so many fruits that he can **draw a brand-new fruit** nobody has seen before. That's **generative AI**.

And if you give him a shopping bag, some money and the list, and he goes and buys everything on his own? That's an **agent**: it doesn't only talk, it gets the job done.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

**Artificial intelligence** is any software that imitates a human ability: understanding language, seeing, listening, predicting, deciding or creating. The terms you'll meet fit inside each other like nested boxes.

| Layer | What it means | Everyday example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Artificial intelligence | Software that behaves intelligently, by rules or by learning | A spam filter, a chess program |
| Machine learning | A model learns patterns from example data | Predicting delivery time from past orders |
| Deep learning | Machine learning with many-layered neural networks | Recognising faces, converting speech to text |
| Generative AI | Deep learning models that create new content from a prompt | Drafting an email, generating an image |
| AI agent | A generative model with instructions and tools that acts on a goal | Booking a meeting room after checking calendars |

### Predict versus create

Traditional machine learning gives you a **prediction**: a number (the price of a flat), a label (spam or not spam) or a group (customers who behave alike). Generative AI gives you **new content**. A large language model, such as the GPT models or Microsoft Phi, writes text by predicting the next small chunk of text again and again. You'll see how that works in the generative AI lesson.

### What makes an agent different

A chat model answers a question. An **agent** is given a goal, **instructions** and **tools** (search your files, run code, call an API). It decides which tool to use, looks at the result and carries on until the task is done. In Microsoft Foundry you build agents with Foundry Agent Service.

### The five AI workloads in AI-901

| Workload | Input → output | Foundry option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generative and agentic AI | Prompt → new content or completed task | Foundry Models, Foundry Agent Service |
| Text analysis | Text → sentiment, key phrases, entities, summary | Azure Language in Foundry Tools, or a language model |
| Speech | Audio ↔ text | Azure Speech in Foundry Tools, audio-capable models |
| Computer vision | Image → description, tags, text; prompt → image | Azure Vision in Foundry Tools, multimodal and image-generation models |
| Information extraction | Documents, images, audio, video → structured fields | Azure Content Understanding in Foundry Tools |

### Where AI-901 fits

AI-901 (Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals) replaced AI-900, which retired on 30 June 2026. It has two domains: **identify AI concepts and capabilities** (40–45%) and **implement AI solutions by using Microsoft Foundry** (55–60%). The second domain is hands-on, so you'll write a little Python against a Foundry project. Microsoft Foundry is the portal at ai.azure.com where you pick models, deploy them, build agents and use Foundry Tools.

<Callout type="exam">
Read the verb in the scenario. "Predict" or "classify" is machine learning, "generate", "draft" or "summarise in your own words" is generative AI, and "take actions" or "use tools to complete a task" is an agent. If the scenario pulls named fields out of an invoice or a call recording, that's information extraction.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Does every AI solution need generative AI?">

No. A sentiment score for a million reviews, a transcript of a call or the total on a receipt can come from a purpose-built Foundry Tool, which is usually cheaper, faster and more predictable than a chat model. Language models can do many of the same jobs through a prompt, and Microsoft is steering several older Language features toward Foundry models over time. A good answer on the exam, and at work, picks the simplest option that meets the requirement.

</DeepDive>

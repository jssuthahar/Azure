## Start with a question

<Story question="You use Copilot Free at home. Your company wants all 40 developers on Copilot. Do they all just sign up for Pro?">

They could, but the company would have no say in how Copilot behaves on its code. No central list of who has it. No way to keep a secrets folder out of it. No audit trail.

That's why Copilot has **plans for individuals** and **plans for organizations**. A person picks Free, Pro or Pro+ for themselves. A company buys **Copilot Business** or **Copilot Enterprise**, assigns **seats** to its people and sets the rules once for everyone.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think about a gym.

You can buy your own pass: a free trial with a few visits a month, a normal pass, or the premium pass.

Or your school buys passes for the whole class. The school decides who gets one and sets the rules, like no phones in the pool.

Copilot works the same way. Your own plan, or one your company gives you.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

### The five plans

Prices below are approximate list prices, converted to your currency (change it in the site settings). Check GitHub's plans page for current pricing.

| Plan | Who buys it | Approx. price | What stands out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Copilot Free** | An individual | Free | Monthly limits on completions and chat |
| **Copilot Pro** | An individual | <span data-usd="10" data-per=" a month">₹880 a month</span> | Unlimited completions, more models, premium request allowance, coding agent |
| **Copilot Pro+** | An individual | <span data-usd="39" data-per=" a month">₹3,432 a month</span> | Everything in Pro, the most models, a bigger premium request allowance, GitHub Spark |
| **Copilot Business** | An organization | <span data-usd="19" data-per=" per user a month">₹1,672 per user a month</span> | Seats, policies, content exclusions, audit log; code not used for training |
| **Copilot Enterprise** | A company on GitHub Enterprise Cloud | <span data-usd="39" data-per=" per user a month">₹3,432 per user a month</span> | Everything in Business plus enterprise features, enterprise-wide policy control and GitHub Spark |

### What an organization plan adds

The jump from individual to organization plans isn't about smarter suggestions. It's about **control**.

- **Seat management.** Admins give Copilot to all members, selected users or teams, or nobody. You pay per assigned seat. A cancelled seat keeps working until the end of the billing cycle. Seats can also be managed with the REST API.
- **Policies.** Turn features on or off for the org: Copilot Chat in the IDE, Copilot on github.com, Copilot CLI, code review, the coding agent, MCP servers, allowed models and whether **suggestions matching public code** are blocked.
- **Content exclusions.** Keep paths or whole repositories out of Copilot's context.
- **Audit log.** Copilot events such as seat changes and policy changes are recorded.
- **Data handling.** On Business and Enterprise, your code isn't used to train GitHub's models.

When you sign in with a Business or Enterprise seat, settings like the public code filter come from your organization and show as read-only in your personal Copilot settings.

### Enterprise on top of Business

An **enterprise** account groups many organizations. It can **enforce** a Copilot policy for all of them, or choose **No policy** so each organization decides. Copilot Enterprise needs **GitHub Enterprise Cloud**.

### Premium requests and budgets

Some models and features count as **premium requests**. Each paid plan includes a monthly allowance. When it runs out, what happens depends on the **budget**: extra usage is billed, or it's blocked until the next month. Admins see seats and usage under **Billing and licensing**.

<Callout type="exam">
Read the buyer in the question. "A developer wants Copilot for personal projects" points to an **individual plan** (Free, Pro or Pro+). "An organization must assign licences, set policies or exclude files" points to **Copilot Business**. "A company using GitHub Enterprise Cloud wants enterprise-wide control" points to **Copilot Enterprise**. Content exclusions and audit log events aren't available on individual plans.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Personal settings versus organization policy">

A developer can use Copilot on personal projects and also hold a seat from an employer. What matters for the exam is where the rules come from. In the organization's context, the organization's Copilot policies decide which features are on and whether suggestions matching public code are blocked, and the developer sees those settings as managed by the org. If the enterprise enforces a policy, the organization can't override it either. Check GitHub's billing docs for how a personal plan is handled once an organization seat is assigned.

</DeepDive>

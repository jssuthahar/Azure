answer: >-
  A **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** is Microsoft's formal commitment to a level of uptime or connectivity for a service, usually as a percentage such as 99.9%, with **service credits** if it is missed. The **service lifecycle** describes how services are released: **private preview**, then **public preview** (usually no SLA), then **general availability (GA)**, which is fully supported with an SLA.

keyTerms:
  - term: SLA
    definition: A formal commitment to uptime or connectivity, with service credits if it is not met.
  - term: Service credit
    definition: A percentage of fees refunded when an SLA is missed.
  - term: Public preview
    definition: A service available to all customers for testing, usually without an SLA.
  - term: General availability (GA)
    definition: A fully released, supported service, normally covered by an SLA.
interview:
  - level: beginner
    q: What is an SLA?
    a: >-
      A promise from Microsoft about how much of the time a service will be available, like 99.9%. If Microsoft misses it, customers can claim service credits.
    followUps: []
  - level: intermediate
    q: What is the difference between public preview and general availability?
    a: >-
      Public preview lets everyone try a new feature, but it usually has no SLA and may change, so it is not recommended for production. General availability means the service is fully released, supported and typically covered by an SLA.
    followUps: []
  - level: experienced
    q: How do you calculate the SLA of an application using several services?
    a: >-
      Multiply the SLAs of services that all must work, which lowers the overall figure. Add redundancy, such as multiple regions, to increase it. Remember SLAs cover the platform, not your own code or deployments.
    followUps: []

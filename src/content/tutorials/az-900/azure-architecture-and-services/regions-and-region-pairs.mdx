## Start with a question

<Story question="If your customers are in India, should your website run on servers in the United States?">

Every message would travel halfway around the world and back, so pages feel slow. And Indian rules for some kinds of data may require it to stay in India.

Azure solves this by having **regions** all over the world. You choose where your resources live.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine a pizza company with kitchens in many cities. You order from the **kitchen closest to you**, so your pizza arrives hot.

Azure regions are Microsoft's "kitchens" around the world. Pick the one near your users and everything is faster. Some kitchens have a **twin** in a nearby city. If one kitchen has a problem, the twin can help.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

An **Azure region** is a set of datacenters deployed within a latency-defined area and connected through a dedicated regional low-latency network. Azure has 60+ regions worldwide, grouped into **geographies** such as Europe, Asia Pacific or the United States, which respect data residency and compliance boundaries.

### Choosing a region

| Factor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Proximity to users | Lower latency, faster apps |
| Data residency and compliance | Some data must stay in a country or geography |
| Service availability | Not every service or VM size is in every region |
| Price | The same service can cost different amounts in different regions |
| Availability zones | Not every region has zones |

### Region pairs

Most regions are paired with another region in the **same geography**, ideally hundreds of kilometres apart:

- **Sequential updates:** planned Azure updates go to one region of a pair at a time.
- **Prioritised recovery:** in a widespread outage, one region in every pair is recovered first.
- **Replication target:** some services, such as geo-redundant storage (GRS), replicate to the paired region.

Some newer regions have **no pair** and rely on availability zones for resilience instead.

### Sovereign regions

**Sovereign regions** are instances of Azure isolated from the main public cloud:

- **Azure Government (US):** for US government agencies and partners, physically isolated and operated by screened US personnel.
- **Azure operated by 21Vianet (China):** Azure in China, operated by a local partner to meet Chinese regulations.

<Callout type="exam">
Know the difference: a **region** is where your resources run, a **region pair** is for disaster recovery and safe updates, and a **sovereign region** is a separate Azure for legal or compliance isolation.
</Callout>

## Start with a question

<Story question="Where does “the cloud” actually live?">

It is not in the sky. The cloud lives in enormous, highly secure buildings full of servers, humming day and night. These are **datacenters**.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine a giant warehouse filled with thousands of fridges, except they are computers. It has its own power plant, giant air conditioners and security guards at every door.

That warehouse is a datacenter. Microsoft has lots of them, and you rent a tiny piece of them whenever you use Azure.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

Azure datacenters are facilities containing racks of servers, storage systems and network equipment, with dedicated power, cooling and networking. Microsoft builds and operates them, and they are protected by layered physical security: perimeter fences, security staff, cameras, biometric access controls and strict processes for destroying old disks.

You do **not** choose a datacenter directly. You choose a **region**, and for zonal services an **availability zone**. Azure decides which datacenter and server your resource runs on.

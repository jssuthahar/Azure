## Start with a question

<Story question="Your company has 50 subscriptions. How do you make sure none of them create resources outside Europe?">

Setting the same rule 50 times is slow and error-prone, and someone will forget. **Management groups** let you set it once, at the top.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

A school has **rules for the whole school** (no running in corridors), and each class follows them automatically. The head teacher doesn't tell every class separately.

A management group is like the whole school. Put a rule there, and every class (subscription) follows it.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

**Management groups** sit above subscriptions in the hierarchy. You can place subscriptions, and other management groups, inside them, building a tree that mirrors how you govern your organisation.

- Policies and RBAC assignments made at a management group are **inherited** by all child management groups, subscriptions, resource groups and resources.
- Every tenant has one **root management group**.
- Management groups can be nested up to **six levels** deep, excluding the root and subscription levels.
- Each management group and subscription has exactly **one parent**.
- A directory can have up to **10,000** management groups.

## Start with a question

<Story question="How does the finance team know which department spent what on Azure?">

If everything is on one bill, nobody knows. Azure's answer is the **subscription**: each one gets its own bill and its own access rules.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of **mobile phone plans**. Each family member can have their own plan with its own bill. Mum can see everyone's bills, but each child only controls their own.

An Azure subscription is like one of those plans: a separate bill and separate rules for what is inside.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

An Azure **subscription** provides authenticated and authorised access to Azure services and is tied to a billing arrangement. It sits between management groups and resource groups in the hierarchy.

| Boundary | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Billing | Each subscription generates its own invoices and cost reports |
| Access control | Apply RBAC roles and Azure Policy to everything in the subscription |

Common reasons for multiple subscriptions: **environments** (prod, dev, test), **organisational structure** (departments or teams), **billing** (cost centres), and **limits** (each subscription has quotas).

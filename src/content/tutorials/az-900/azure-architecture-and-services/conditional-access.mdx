  **Microsoft Entra Conditional Access** is a tool that uses **signals**, such as who the user is, their location, device, the app they want and sign-in risk, to make an access **decision**: allow access, require MFA or another control, or block access. It works like an "if this, then that" policy for sign-ins.

  - term: Conditional Access policy

  - term: Signal
    definition: Information used in the decision, such as user, location, device, app or risk.
  - term: Grant control
    definition: The requirement or decision, such as require MFA, require a compliant device, or block.
interview:
  - level: beginner
    q: What is Conditional Access?
    a: >-
      A set of rules in Microsoft Entra ID that decides how someone can sign in based on conditions. For example, if they sign in from outside the office, they must use MFA.
    followUps: []
  - level: intermediate
    q: Give three example Conditional Access policies.
    a: >-
      Require MFA for all administrators. Block sign-ins from countries where the company doesn't operate. Require a compliant or company-managed device to access sensitive apps.
    followUps: []
  - level: experienced
    q: How do you roll out Conditional Access safely?
    a: >-
      Start in report-only mode to see the impact, exclude emergency break-glass accounts, target pilot groups first, use the What If tool, and monitor sign-in logs before switching policies on for everyone.

## Start with a question

<Story question="Should signing in from your office desk be treated the same as signing in from an unknown country at 3 a.m.?">

Of course not. **Conditional Access** looks at the situation and decides what to ask for.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

A smart school gate: if you're a student arriving at 8 a.m., it opens. If it's midnight, it asks for a teacher's permission. If it doesn't recognise you, it stays shut.

</Eli10>

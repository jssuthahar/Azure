## Start with a question

<Story question="What if the building that holds your servers loses power?">

Even the best datacenter can lose power, overheat or have a network fault. If all your servers are in that one building, your app stops.

Availability zones let you put copies of your app in **separate buildings in the same city**, so one building's bad day does not become yours.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think about keeping your favourite toys at **three different friends' houses** in the same town. If one house has a power cut, you can still play at the other two.

Availability zones are those three houses: close enough to talk to each other quickly, far enough apart that one problem does not affect them all.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

An **availability zone** is a physically separate location within an Azure region, made up of one or more datacenters with **independent power, cooling and networking**. Zones in a region are connected by high-speed, low-latency private fibre, so data can be replicated between them almost instantly. A zone-enabled region has a **minimum of three zones**.

| Service category | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zonal | You choose the zone for each resource | A VM in zone 2 |
| Zone-redundant | Azure replicates across zones for you | Zone-redundant storage (ZRS), zone-redundant Azure SQL Database |
| Non-regional | Always available, not tied to a region | Microsoft Entra ID, Azure DNS |

<Callout type="exam">
Zones protect you from a **datacenter** failure. For a whole-**region** failure, you need a second region, often the region pair.
</Callout>

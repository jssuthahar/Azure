## Start with a question

<Story question="When you send an email, do you ever think about the mail server?">

You open Outlook or Gmail, type, press send. You never installed a mail server, never patched one, never bought a disk to store your messages. Someone else does all of that.

That everyday experience is **Software as a Service**, the most complete form of cloud.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Some days you cook at home. Some days your family orders **pizza delivery**. With delivery you don't buy ingredients, own an oven or wash the pans. The pizza simply arrives, ready to eat.

SaaS is software delivered ready to use. **You just open it and use it.** Somebody else does all the cooking and cleaning behind the scenes.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

With **Software as a Service**, the provider hosts and manages the **entire application stack**: datacenter, hardware, OS, runtime, the application itself, and its updates. Customers access it through a browser or app, usually paying a **subscription per user or per month**.

What you can still do is **configure**: create users, set permissions, choose security policies, adjust workflows and integrate through APIs. What you can't do is change the vendor's code or infrastructure.

| You manage | The provider manages |
| --- | --- |
| Your data (what you store, classify and share) | The application and its updates |
| User accounts and identities | Runtime, OS, middleware |
| Devices that access the service | Servers, storage, networking |
| Access policies (for example MFA, sharing settings) | Datacenter and physical security |

### Examples

- **Microsoft 365**: Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel, SharePoint, OneDrive.
- **Dynamics 365**: CRM and ERP business applications.
- Many others from different vendors: online accounting, HR systems, ticketing tools, video conferencing.

<Callout type="warning">
A common mistake is believing SaaS means "security is the vendor's problem". In the shared responsibility model, **data, devices, and accounts and identities are always the customer's responsibility**, even in SaaS. Turning on multifactor authentication matters more than anything else you can do.
</Callout>

  industry="Healthcare"

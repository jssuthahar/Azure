## Start with a question

<Story question="Why does a train ticket bought months ahead cost less than one bought at the station?">

Railways reward you for **committing early**. They can plan capacity, so they give you a discount. Buy at the last minute and you pay full price, but you keep total flexibility. Some railways even sell cheap "standby" tickets: travel only if there's a spare seat.

Azure prices compute in almost exactly the same way.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine a **bowling alley**:

- 🎳 **Pay per game** whenever you show up: easy, but full price. (Pay-as-you-go)
- 📅 **Buy a season pass** for a whole year: much cheaper per game, but you promised to keep coming. (Reservation)
- 🪑 **Standby lane**: super cheap, but if a big group arrives they can ask you to stop. (Spot)
- 🎟️ **Bring your old tokens** from another alley that accepts them. (Hybrid Benefit)

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

The consumption-based model tells you *that* you pay for usage. **Pricing models** decide *how much* each unit costs, depending on how much flexibility you're willing to give up.

| Model | Commitment | Discount (vs pay-as-you-go) | Flexibility | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pay-as-you-go** | None | None (standard rate) | Highest | New, variable or short-lived workloads |
| **Reservations** | 1 or 3 years, specific resource type/region | Large (Microsoft cites up to 72% for some VMs) | Lowest (exchanges are possible within rules) | Stable, predictable workloads |
| **Savings plan for compute** | 1 or 3 years, fixed hourly spend | Significant (Microsoft cites up to 65%) | Applies across sizes, regions and eligible services | Steady spend with changing shapes |
| **Spot** | None | Deep (up to 90% at times) | Can be evicted at short notice | Batch, rendering, CI, dev/test |
| **Azure Hybrid Benefit** | Requires eligible existing licences | Removes licence cost | Combines with the models above | Windows Server and SQL Server workloads |

<Callout type="note">
Discount percentages vary by service, size, region and term, and Microsoft changes them over time. Always check current numbers in the **Azure pricing calculator** before quoting them.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="How commitments are applied on your bill">

Reservations and savings plans are **billing constructs**, not resources. You don't deploy "a reserved VM". You buy a reservation, and the discount automatically applies each hour to matching running resources in the chosen scope (a resource group, a subscription, a management group or the whole billing account).

- If nothing matching runs in a given hour, that hour of commitment is **lost**.
- Savings plan benefit applies hour by hour to the usage with the biggest discount first, then any overflow is billed at pay-as-you-go.
- **Instance size flexibility** lets a reservation cover other sizes in the same VM series.
- Microsoft also offers a **savings plan for databases** (Azure SQL, Cosmos DB, PostgreSQL, MySQL and others), with smaller discounts than the compute plan.
- Watch **utilisation reports** in Cost Management. Below roughly 90–100% utilisation, you're paying for nothing.

</DeepDive>

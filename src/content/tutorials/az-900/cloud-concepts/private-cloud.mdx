    a: >-

## Start with a question

<Story question="Why would a bank or a defence company pay more to have a cloud all to itself?">

Shared bus networks are cheap and convenient, yet some people still buy a private car, or even an armoured one. They want to decide exactly who gets in, where it is parked and who services it.

Some organisations feel the same about their computing. They want the **convenience of cloud** (click a button, get a server) without **sharing hardware** with anyone. That is a private cloud.

</Story>

## The technical explanation

A **private cloud** is a cloud deployment model where the infrastructure is **provisioned for exclusive use by a single organisation**. It can be:

- **On-premises**: in the organisation's own datacenter, run by its own staff, or
- **Hosted**: in a third-party datacenter, on hardware dedicated to that organisation.

What makes it a *cloud*, not just a server room, is that it offers cloud characteristics to its internal users: **self-service provisioning**, **pooled and virtualised resources**, **automation** and often **usage metering and chargeback** to departments.

| | Private cloud | Public cloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tenancy | Single organisation | Many organisations (isolated) |
| Up-front cost | High (hardware, software, facilities) | None |
| Hardware maintenance | Organisation | Provider |
| Scalability | Limited by purchased hardware | Near-unlimited |
| Control | Maximum | Configuration-level |
| Typical drivers | Strict compliance, data control, legacy integration | Agility, scale, cost |

<Callout type="exam">
AZ-900 tests your ability to match a scenario to a model. If the scenario says the organisation **must maintain its own hardware** or needs **complete control**, the answer is usually private cloud. If it says **no capital expenditure**, it is public cloud.
</Callout>

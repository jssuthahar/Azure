## Start with a question

<Story question="Should a new bakery buy a 30,000 oven on day one, or rent one monthly until it knows how busy it will be?">

Buying the oven means a huge payment before the first loaf is sold. The bakery owns it, but if the business changes direction, the oven is still there, losing value.

Renting means smaller monthly payments, no big risk, and the freedom to upgrade or return it. **These are the two ways businesses spend money: CapEx and OpEx.**

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine you want to read lots of comics.

- **CapEx** is spending all your birthday money at once to **buy** a big pile of comics. They are yours forever, but you cannot buy anything else this month, and you may get bored of some of them.
- **OpEx** is paying a little bit **every week** for a comic library membership. You read what you want and can stop paying whenever you like.

The cloud is the library membership.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

| | Capital expenditure (CapEx) | Operational expenditure (OpEx) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Up-front spending on physical infrastructure or assets | Ongoing spending on services or products |
| Example in IT | Buying servers, storage, networking, building a datacenter | Cloud subscriptions, pay-as-you-go VMs, SaaS licences |
| Accounting | Asset is depreciated over its useful life (often 3–5 years for IT) | Expensed in the period it occurs |
| Cash flow | Large payment at the start | Smaller, regular payments that follow usage |
| Flexibility | Low. You own fixed capacity | High. Scale up or down, stop anytime |
| Risk | Over-buying (idle assets) or under-buying (capacity shortage) | Uncontrolled usage can grow costs |

### Why the cloud is OpEx

Because Azure is billed through the **consumption-based model**, you do not buy the infrastructure. You pay monthly for what you use. That shifts IT spending from CapEx to OpEx, which:

- removes the big up-front payment,
- lets costs follow business demand,
- and avoids being stuck with assets you no longer need.

<Callout type="exam">
AZ-900 questions often hide this concept inside a scenario: "The company wants to avoid a large initial investment in hardware" → cloud / OpEx. "The company purchases servers for its datacenter" → CapEx.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Commitments blur the line">

Reservations and savings plans are paid like OpEx (monthly or up front) but **behave like a commitment**: you owe the money whether or not you use the capacity. Finance teams sometimes treat large up-front reservation payments as prepaid expenses spread over the term. For architecture discussions, the useful question is not "CapEx or OpEx?" but **"How much flexibility are we buying, and what does it cost?"**

</DeepDive>

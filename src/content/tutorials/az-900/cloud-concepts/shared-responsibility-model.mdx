## Start with a question

<Story question="If someone breaks into your rented flat because you left the door unlocked, is that the landlord's fault?">

The landlord is responsible for the building: solid walls, a working front-door lock, a safe staircase. **You** are responsible for locking your own door and not giving your key to strangers.

Cloud security works exactly like this. Many people assume "it is in the cloud, so Microsoft keeps it safe". Microsoft keeps the building safe. **Locking your door is still your job.**

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

At a **school sleepover in a hotel**, the hotel makes sure the building is safe: fire alarms, locked main doors, security guards. Your teacher makes sure the right children are in the right rooms. And **you** look after your own bag and don't tell anyone your room code.

In the cloud, Microsoft is the hotel, your company's IT team is the teacher, and every user is responsible for their own password. **Everyone has a job. Safety only works if everyone does theirs.**

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

The **shared responsibility model** divides security and operational duties between the provider and the customer. A useful summary is:

- The provider is responsible for **security *of* the cloud** (facilities, hardware, network, virtualisation, and the managed parts of its services).
- The customer is responsible for **security *in* the cloud** (what they deploy, configure and store).

How much each side does depends on the service type. Read the chart from left to right: as you move from on-premises to SaaS, more rows turn blue (Microsoft).

<ResponsibilityGrid />

### The three rules to remember

1. **Always Microsoft** (in any Azure model): physical datacenter, physical network, physical hosts.
2. **Always you**: information and data; devices (mobile and PCs); accounts and identities.
3. **It depends**: identity and directory infrastructure, applications, network controls and the operating system shift towards Microsoft as you move from IaaS to PaaS to SaaS.

<DeepDive title="Turning customer responsibilities into controls">

| Customer responsibility | Azure controls that help |
| --- | --- |
| Accounts and identities | Microsoft Entra ID, MFA, Conditional Access, Privileged Identity Management, managed identities |
| Data | Encryption at rest (on by default) with optional customer-managed keys in Key Vault, Microsoft Purview for classification |
| Network controls | NSGs, Azure Firewall, private endpoints, DDoS Protection |
| OS (IaaS) | Azure Update Manager, Defender for Servers, just-in-time VM access |
| Configuration posture | Azure Policy, Defender for Cloud secure score and recommendations |

The model also applies to **resilience**, not only security. Microsoft keeps the platform available within the SLA. Designing your application across zones, backing up your data and testing restores are your job.

</DeepDive>

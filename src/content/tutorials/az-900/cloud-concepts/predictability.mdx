## Start with a question

<Story question="Would you trust a bus company if the bus sometimes takes 10 minutes and sometimes 2 hours, and the fare changes every day?">

Even if the bus *usually* arrives, you can't plan your life around it. Businesses feel the same about technology. They need to know **how it will perform** and **what it will cost** before they commit to it.

That confidence is called **predictability**.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think about your **pocket money**. If you know you get the same amount every week, you can plan: "In three weeks I can buy that game." And if your bike always gets you to school in 10 minutes, you know when to leave home.

The cloud helps companies know **how fast** their apps will be and **how much** they will pay, so they can plan ahead just like you.

</Eli10>

### Tools that support predictability

| Need | Azure tool |
| --- | --- |
| Estimate costs before building | Azure pricing calculator |
| Track, analyse and forecast actual spend | Microsoft Cost Management (cost analysis, budgets, alerts) |
| Lock in lower prices for steady usage | Reservations, savings plans |
| Keep performance consistent under load | Autoscale, load balancers, caching |
| Get best-practice recommendations | Azure Advisor, Azure Well-Architected Framework |
| Prevent expensive surprises | Azure Policy (for example, allowed VM sizes and regions) |

<Callout type="tip">
The **Azure Well-Architected Framework** is Microsoft's design guidance for good workloads. Its five pillars are **Reliability, Security, Cost Optimization, Operational Excellence and Performance Efficiency**. It isn't required for AZ-900, but it's excellent interview vocabulary.
</Callout>

## When to use it and when not to

Predictability is a goal, not a single service. Prioritise it when:

<UseWhen
  use={[
    'You must commit prices to customers or budgets to management.',
    'User experience has to be consistent (customer-facing apps, SLAs to your clients).',
    'Your workloads have known patterns you can plan capacity and commitments for.',
  ]}
  avoid={[
    'Over-optimising predictability for early experiments. Flexibility matters more at that stage.',
    'Locking into long commitments before you understand usage.',
  ]}

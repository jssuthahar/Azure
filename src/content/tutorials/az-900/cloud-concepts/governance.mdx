    q: How do you build governance that does not slow developers down?
    a: >-
      Use guardrails rather than gates. Design a management group hierarchy (for example from Azure landing zones), assign policy initiatives at the top so every subscription inherits them, use deny only for the critical rules (public exposure, allowed regions), use audit and deployIfNotExists for the rest so things are fixed automatically, and give teams self-service subscriptions inside those boundaries. Make compliance visible on a dashboard and treat policy as code in a repository with reviews.

## Start with a question

<Story question="What stops an employee from creating a 50,000-a-month server in the wrong country on a Friday afternoon?">

In many organisations, the honest answer used to be "nothing, until the bill arrives". With hundreds of people able to create resources in minutes, you need rules, and those rules must be checked automatically. Nobody can review every click by hand.

That is **governance**.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Every school has **rules**: wear your uniform, no running in the corridor, phones off in class. Teachers check that the rules are followed.

Now imagine a **magic school** where the door simply won't open if you aren't wearing your uniform, and a friendly robot fixes your tie for you. In the cloud, **rules can be checked and even enforced automatically**. That is cloud governance.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

Microsoft's AZ-900 material describes how the cloud supports governance and compliance:

1. **Templates** help you deploy resources that already meet corporate standards and regulatory requirements.
2. **Auditing** can automatically flag resources that are out of compliance.
3. **Updates and enforcement**: providers update the platform with new compliance capabilities, and policies can automatically block or remediate non-compliant resources.

### The governance toolbox in Azure

| Tool | Governs | Covered in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Azure Policy** | What resources may look like (regions, SKUs, tags, encryption) | Module 3 |
| **Resource locks** | Prevent accidental deletion or modification | Module 3 |
| **Management groups** | Apply policies and access across many subscriptions | Module 2 |
| **Tags** | Ownership, environment and cost centre metadata | Module 3 |
| **Microsoft Purview** | Data governance: discover, classify and track data | Module 3 |
| **Azure RBAC** | Who can do what (access governance) | Module 2 |
| **Cost Management** | Budgets and spending accountability | Module 3 |

<Callout type="exam">
A classic trap is **Azure Policy vs Azure RBAC**. RBAC answers **who** can create a VM. Policy answers **which kind** of VM may be created (for example, only certain sizes in certain regions).
</Callout>

## Real-world business scenario

<Scenario
  company="A multinational pharmaceutical company"
  industry="Healthcare / life sciences"
  problem="Research teams created resources in many regions without tags; auditors could not prove where clinical data lived or who owned which costs."
  service="Management group hierarchy with Azure Policy initiatives (allowed EU regions, mandatory owner and cost-centre tags, encryption required), resource locks on production data stores, and Microsoft Purview to classify sensitive data."
  why="Rules are enforced automatically at creation time and continuously audited, without blocking teams' self-service."
  outcome="Compliance reports are generated from the dashboard in minutes, and untagged spending dropped to near zero."
/>

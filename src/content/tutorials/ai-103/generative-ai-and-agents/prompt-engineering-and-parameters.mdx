## Start with a question

<Story question="Why do two cooks make different dishes from the same recipe?">

One recipe says "season to taste". One cook adds a pinch, another adds a fistful. One follows the recipe card exactly, another improvises. If you want the same dish every time, you write clearer steps, show a photo of the finished plate and tell them how much to use.

A model is the cook. **Instructions** are the recipe. A **worked example** is the photo. **Temperature** is how much the cook improvises. **Max output tokens** is the size of the plate.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Imagine asking a friend to tell a story.

If you say "tell it exactly like the book", they copy the book. That's a **low temperature**.

If you say "make it wild!", the story gets silly and different every time. That's a **high temperature**.

If you only give them one minute, the story stops mid-sentence. That's **max output tokens** too low.

And if they read their story back and fix the mistakes before telling you, that's **self-critique**.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

### 1. Prompt: say it clearly

A good instruction names the **role**, the **audience**, the **rules**, the **format** and the **length**.

```text
You are Contoso's product copywriter. Write a product description for an Indian online shop.
Use plain English, mention the warranty, and stay under 60 words.

Example
Input: steel water bottle
Output: Keeps chai hot for 12 hours. Leak-proof lid, 1-year warranty.
```

Put separate parts in clear sections, give the answer format, and add **one or two examples** when the format matters. That's **few-shot prompting**.

### 2. Parameters: shape the sampling

| Setting | Effect | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperature** | Low is steady, high is varied | 0 to 0.3 for facts and extraction, higher for ideas |
| **Top P** | Limits to the most likely words | Change it **or** temperature, not both |
| **Max output tokens** | Caps reply length | Too low cuts the answer. Check the status |

```python
r = openai.responses.create(model="support-chat", instructions=instructions, input=product,
                            temperature=0.2, top_p=1, max_output_tokens=300)
print(r.output_text, r.status, r.incomplete_details)   # status "incomplete" means a cap was hit
```

**Reasoning models** such as o4-mini don't take temperature. You steer them with **reasoning effort** (low, medium or high), and a higher effort costs more tokens and time.

### 3. Reflection and self-critique

Let the model check its work. Draft, then critique against a checklist, then revise once:

```python
draft = openai.responses.create(model=m, instructions=instructions, input=product).output_text
critique = openai.responses.create(model=m, input=f"Check this against the rules and list problems: {draft}").output_text
final = openai.responses.create(model=m, input=f"Rewrite fixing: {critique}\n{draft}").output_text
```

Keep the checklist short and the loop to **one or two rounds**. It costs extra calls, so measure whether it raises evaluation scores. For reasoning, a **chain-of-thought** style prompt ("work through the steps, then answer") can help standard models on multistep problems. Reasoning models think by themselves, so you don't need to ask. You can evaluate the reasoning by scoring intermediate steps against a rubric, or by comparing several attempts and keeping the consistent answer.

<Callout type="exam">
Match the symptom to the knob. "Different wording every time" points to a **lower temperature**. "Wrong format" points to a **few-shot example**. "Ends mid-sentence with status incomplete" points to **max output tokens**. "A reasoning model rejects temperature" points to **reasoning effort**. "Draft, check, revise" is **self-critique**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Change one thing at a time">

If you change the prompt, the temperature and the model together, you won't know which helped. Change one setting, run your evaluation set, and keep the change only if scores improve. Prompts are code: store them in Git, version them, and compare versions on the same data.

</DeepDive>

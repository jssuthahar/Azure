## Start with a question

<Story question="You send a friend a photo of a dented car door and ask, 'Which door is it?' Why does your friend answer so quickly?">

They look at the photo, find the dent and tell you. If you ask, "How old is the car?" they'll say, "I can't tell from this picture."

That's the behaviour you want from a **multimodal model**. It reads the image and your question together, answers from what it sees, and says so when the photo doesn't show the answer.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

A multimodal model is a helper who can read words and look at pictures at the same time.

You hand over a photo and a note. If the note says "one short sentence", you get one short sentence. If it says "tell me everything", you get a long story.

A good helper also says "I can't see that" instead of making something up.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

Pick a deployment whose model takes **image input**, such as **gpt-4.1-mini**. A text-only deployment returns an error when you attach a picture.

### Send an image

```python
response = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="gpt-4.1-mini",                      # deployment name
    messages=[
        {"role": "system", "content": "Answer only from what is visible. If the image does not show it, say you can't tell."},
        {"role": "user", "content": [
            {"type": "text", "text": "Which door is damaged?"},
            {"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": data_url, "detail": "high"}},
        ]},
    ],
)
print(response.choices[0].message.content)
```

`url` can be a web address or a **base64 data URL**. Add more `image_url` parts to send several images (up to **10** in one request).

| Detail | What the model sees | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `low` | A reduced version | Fast, cheap captions and big objects |
| `high` | More of the full image | Small text, tiny objects |
| `auto` | The service decides | A sensible default |

### Four jobs, four prompts

| Job | What the prompt asks for |
| --- | --- |
| **Concise caption** | "One sentence, under 20 words, no opinions." Good for galleries and notifications. |
| **Detailed caption** | "Describe layout, colours, objects, readable text and anything unusual." Good for reports and search. |
| **Grounded Q&A** | "Answer only from what is visible. If it isn't shown, say you can't tell." |
| **Alt text** | Purpose first, short, read out meaningful text, no "image of", empty for decoration. |

With **several images** in one request, the model can also compare them: "Which of these photos shows damage?"

### Alt text and extended descriptions

Alt text is for people who can't see the image. Keep it to the point, around a sentence. A chart or a floor plan often needs more. Put the short version in `alt` and offer an **extended description** in the page text or a linked page. The guidance comes from accessibility standards such as WCAG, and your prompt is where you apply it.

### Locating things

You can ask the model to return JSON with a label and a box for each object, as fractions of the image width and height. Treat the boxes as **approximate**. Draw them for a person to confirm, or use Content Understanding when you need repeatable fields.

<Callout type="exam">
Three signals to watch for. "Answer only from the image" means a **grounding** instruction. "Screen reader" means **alt text**. "Small text is missed" means **detail high**, not a bigger model or a higher temperature.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="What if the photo contains text that gives orders?">

The model reads that text like any other part of the picture, and it might obey it. That's an indirect prompt injection, and it gets its own lesson in this module. For now remember one rule: text inside an image is **data**, not a command.

</DeepDive>

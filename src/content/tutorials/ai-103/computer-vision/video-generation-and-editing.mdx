## Start with a question

<Story question="You ordered a food delivery, and the app says 'in progress'. Why doesn't the pizza arrive the moment you tap the button?">

Because cooking takes time. The app gives you an order number, you check the status now and then, and you collect the pizza when it's ready.

Generating a video works the same way. You don't get the video back in the reply. You get a **video ID**, you **poll** the job, and you **download** the file when its status says **completed**.

</Story>

## Explain it like I'm 10

<Eli10>

Think of a robot that makes a tiny movie when you describe it.

It needs a few minutes, so it gives you a ticket. You come back with the ticket and ask, "Is it ready?" When it is, you get the movie.

If you like the movie but want a sunnier sky, you give the robot the old ticket and say, "Same movie, but sunnier." That's a **remix**.

</Eli10>

## The technical explanation

Deploy **sora-2** on your Foundry resource. At the time of writing (25 November 2026) it's a **preview** model, so confirm the regions and deployment type on Microsoft Learn.

### Create, poll, download

```python
import time
video = client.videos.create(
    model="sora-2",                       # deployment name
    prompt="A brass tea kettle on a wooden table, steam rising, soft morning light, slow push-in",
    size="1280x720", seconds="8",
    input_reference=open("product-1280x720.png", "rb"),   # optional
)
while video.status in ("queued", "in_progress"):
    time.sleep(20)
    video = client.videos.retrieve(video.id)
if video.status == "completed":
    client.videos.download_content(video.id).write_to_file("kettle.mp4")
```

| Control | Values and rules |
| --- | --- |
| `size` | 720x1280 (portrait) or 1280x720 (landscape) |
| `seconds` | 4, 8 or 12 |
| `input_reference` | Same size as the video. No human faces |
| Prompt | Shot, subject, action, setting, light, camera movement |
| Status | queued, in_progress, completed |

The prompt does the creative work. A good one reads like a shot list: who or what is in frame, what moves, where, in what light, and how the camera behaves. Prompts that name real people or protected characters are rejected.

### Edit with remix

```python
remixed = client.videos.remix(
    video.id,
    prompt="Change the morning light to warm sunset light.",
)
```

**Remix** starts from a completed video, so everything you don't mention stays close to the original. Ask for one change. If you pile on five, the result drifts, and you may be better off generating again.

<Callout type="exam">
"The application must edit a video that was already generated" points to **remix**, not a new create call. "The request must not block" points to the **asynchronous** pattern. "The reference image causes an error" points to **size** and **faces**.
</Callout>

<DeepDive title="Where do the finished videos live?">

The service stores jobs and outputs for you, but treat that as temporary. Download the file into your own Blob Storage with your own retention, and delete jobs you don't need. Generated videos can carry provenance information such as content credentials, so keep that metadata when you publish.

</DeepDive>
